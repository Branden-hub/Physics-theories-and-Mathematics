$$

$$
P(X_1, X_2, ..., X_n) = \prod_{i=1}^n P(X_i)

### **2. Interaction Information and Dependency**

Where $$ I(X;Y) $$ is the mutual information between $$ X $$ and $$ Y $$, and $$ I(X;Y|Z) $$ is the conditional mutual information given $$ Z $$. This generalizes to more variables and can be used to construct *hypergraphs* that map out higher-order dependencies[2].

### **3. Supermodularity and Interdependence**

In economics and systems theory, *supermodular functions* are used to capture the idea that the value (or output) of the system increases when variables are more aligned or interdependent. Mathematically, a function $$ f $$ is supermodular if:

$$
f(x \vee y) + f(x \wedge y) \geq f(x) + f(y)
$$

for all $$ x, y $$. This property allows comparison and quantification of interdependence among variables[5].
